## 2026-10-26: Proposal: 90th-percentile Midtown-to-JFK taxi time

- Decision. Estimate the conditional 90th percentile of metered trip duration, in minutes, for yellow-taxi rides from Midtown (pickup zones 161, 162, 163, 230) to JFK (zone 132), using TLC records from January–March 2024. Compare quantile regression on pickup hour, day of week, and pickup zone against a fixed January 90th-percentile allowance. Fit both on January, calibrate both on February with an additive offset (the 90th percentile of February errors, actual minus predicted), freeze both, and score them on March. Score with quantile loss L(B, T) = 0.9 max(T − B, 0) + 0.1 max(B − T, 0). The primary outcome is March mean quantile loss. A 95% target is a sensitivity analysis.
- Why. A traveler reserving time for the road to the airport loses more by arriving late than by waiting at the gate, so the allowance should be an upper quantile, not an average. The 0.9/0.1 weights make a minute short count nine times a minute of unused padding. A preliminary download checked 24 October 2026 found 21,725 positive-duration candidate trips, enough for one month to fit and one month each to calibrate and test. The split runs forward in time, so March is never seen before scoring. Predictors are limited to what is known before departure.
- Alternative. Predicting mean or median trip duration with squared or absolute error. Rejected because a central estimate is late about half the time, and that is the costly mistake. Also rejected: using trip distance, fare, tips, or drop-off time as predictors (not known before departure), and dropping the 37 rides over six hours because they hurt performance (they will be investigated and handled in a sensitivity analysis instead).
- Revisit. If February calibration cannot bring either method near 90% coverage, or if coverage differs sharply across departure periods, reconsider the predictors or the quantile. If investigating the duplicates and long rides changes the sample materially, record a new entry for the data. If the fixed allowance wins on March, report that result; do not change the method after seeing March.

## 2026-11-01: Initial January data check

- Decision. Document the initial January 2024 Midtown-to-JFK data check in `analysis.ipynb` (Data section), with the histogram saved as `fig/january_duration_histogram.png`. Display durations from 0–150 minutes for readability while retaining the 20 trips (0.29%) beyond that range in the complete data and summary statistics. Retain flagged records pending investigation.
- Why. The selected sample contains 6,885 trips, with a median duration of 49.98 minutes and a 90th percentile of 71.18 minutes. There are 13 records over six hours and 110 rows in possible duplicate groups. These need investigation before modeling.

- Revisit. Agree on and document the treatment of questionable records before fitting the baseline and model. February remains the calibration period, and March remains the final test period.

## 2026-11-07: Special events and non-recurrent traffic (issue #3)

- Decision. The allowance is meant for ordinary days. A day is flagged if, by 6 pm the day before, it met one of three rules, each from an outside source: (R1) a US federal holiday; (R2) a publicly announced event with street closures inside pickup zones 161, 162, 163 or 230, or on FDR Drive; (R3) a National Weather Service Winter Storm, Blizzard, Ice Storm, Flood or Flash Flood Warning for Manhattan or Queens, not a watch or an advisory. Whole days, by pickup date; the same rules and sources for all three months; a day whose status cannot be confirmed is ordinary. The rules flag seven days: 1 and 15 January (R1), 13 February (R3, Winter Storm Warning issued at 4 pm on 12 February) and 19 February (R1), and 16, 17 and 28 March (R2: the St Patrick's Day parade, the NYC Half Marathon, and the presidential fundraiser at Radio City). `events.py` lists these and every rejected candidate with its source. Flagged days are removed from the January fit and the February offset, for the model and the fixed allowance alike. March is scored on ordinary days and on all days, reported side by side, and on its flagged days alone as counts and rates. Unannounced disruptions (crashes, breakdowns, the 8 January bridge and tunnel protests) are not flagged: they are not known before departure, and the 90th percentile is meant to cover them. The 19–23 February school recess is ordinary.
- Why. Issue #3 asked how special events and non-recurrent traffic enter the forecast, or which days the allowance is not meant to cover, and that March not treat those days as ordinary residuals. Event predictors cannot be estimated: January and February have two flagged days each, and March's events have no counterpart in them. On January and February the flagged days hold 629 and 428 trips and run faster than ordinary days (daily medians 34 to 43 minutes against 54 on a typical ordinary day; the 13 February snow day, with schools remote, had a 90th percentile of 44.6 minutes), so leaving them in would pull the allowance down for ordinary days. Flood warnings are issued while rain falls, so none met the 6 pm cut-off (9 January and 23 March were rejected for that reason); in practice R3 flags winter storms only. Coastal flood and high wind warnings are not R3 types, so 9–10 and 13 January and 10 March stay ordinary. A draft analysis outside this repository had already scored March; its worst days were 8, 9 and 16 March. The rules flag 16 March (the parade) but not 8 or 9 March, which stay ordinary, and they flag 17 and 28 March, which were not among the worst. The rules were written down before the dates were looked up, and this entry is merged before March is scored in this repository.
- Alternative. Holiday, event and weather predictors in the quantile regression (too few flagged days to estimate them). Keeping flagged days in the fit and offset and reporting them only in the March score (they would shift the allowance for ordinary days). Event hours instead of whole days (closure times are not published for every event). Observed weather instead of warnings (not known before departure). Counting watches and advisories as well as warnings (a looser rule that would flag 16 and 19 January, 17 February, and 6–7 and 23 March). Counting coastal flood and high wind warnings (they would flag 9–10 and 13 January and 10 March; R3 was written for snow, ice and flooding rain).
- Revisit. R2 events were found from NYC closure notices and news coverage, not from a complete register of permits. If a source shows a January–March 2024 event or warning that meets a rule and is missing from `events.py`, add it in a new entry before March is scored. If the March results on flagged days are too few to say anything, report the counts and say so.
