## 2026-10-26: Proposal: 90th-percentile Midtown-to-JFK taxi time

- Decision. Estimate the conditional 90th percentile of metered trip duration, in minutes, for yellow-taxi rides from Midtown (pickup zones 161, 162, 163, 230) to JFK (zone 132), using TLC records from January–March 2024. Compare quantile regression on pickup hour, day of week, and pickup zone against a fixed January 90th-percentile allowance. Fit both on January, calibrate both on February with an additive offset (the 90th percentile of February errors, actual minus predicted), freeze both, and score them on March. Score with quantile loss L(B, T) = 0.9 max(T − B, 0) + 0.1 max(B − T, 0). The primary outcome is March mean quantile loss. A 95% target is a sensitivity analysis.
- Why. A traveler reserving time for the road to the airport loses more by arriving late than by waiting at the gate, so the allowance should be an upper quantile, not an average. The 0.9/0.1 weights make a minute short count nine times a minute of unused padding. A preliminary download checked 24 October 2026 found 21,725 positive-duration candidate trips, enough for one month to fit and one month each to calibrate and test. The split runs forward in time, so March is never seen before scoring. Predictors are limited to what is known before departure.
- Alternative. Predicting mean or median trip duration with squared or absolute error. Rejected because a central estimate is late about half the time, and that is the costly mistake. Also rejected: using trip distance, fare, tips, or drop-off time as predictors (not known before departure), and dropping the 37 rides over six hours because they hurt performance (they will be investigated and handled in a sensitivity analysis instead).
- Revisit. If February calibration cannot bring either method near 90% coverage, or if coverage differs sharply across departure periods, reconsider the predictors or the quantile. If investigating the duplicates and long rides changes the sample materially, record a new entry for the data. If the fixed allowance wins on March, report that result; do not change the method after seeing March.

## 2026-11-07: Special events and non-recurrent traffic (issue #3)

- Decision. The allowance is meant for ordinary days. A day is flagged if, by 6 pm the day before, it met one of three rules, each from an outside source: (R1) a US federal holiday; (R2) a publicly announced event with street closures inside pickup zones 161, 162, 163 or 230, or on FDR Drive; (R3) a National Weather Service Winter Storm, Blizzard, Ice Storm, Flood or Flash Flood Warning for Manhattan or Queens, not a watch or an advisory. Whole days, by pickup date; the same rules and sources for all three months; a day whose status cannot be confirmed is ordinary. The rules flag six days: 1 and 15 January (R1), 13 February (R3, Winter Storm Warning issued at 4 pm on 12 February) and 19 February (R1), and 16 and 17 March (R2: the St Patrick's Day parade and the NYC Half Marathon). The presidential fundraiser at Radio City on 28 March is ordinary: its date was public weeks ahead, but the earliest notice of street closures found was published at 8:30 am that day, after the cut-off. `events.py` lists these and every rejected candidate with its source. Flagged days are removed from the January fit and the February offset, for the model and the fixed allowance alike. March is scored on ordinary days and on all days, reported side by side, and on its flagged days alone as counts and rates. Unannounced disruptions (crashes, breakdowns, the 8 January bridge and tunnel protests) are not flagged: they are not known before departure, and the 90th percentile is meant to cover them. The 19–23 February school recess is ordinary.
- Why. Issue #3 asked how special events and non-recurrent traffic enter the forecast, or which days the allowance is not meant to cover, and that March not treat those days as ordinary residuals. Event predictors cannot be estimated: January and February have two flagged days each, and March's events have no counterpart in them. On January and February the flagged days hold 629 and 428 trips and run faster than ordinary days (daily medians 34 to 43 minutes against 54 on a typical ordinary day; the 13 February snow day, with schools remote, had a 90th percentile of 44.6 minutes), so leaving them in would pull the allowance down for ordinary days. Flood warnings are issued while rain falls, so none met the 6 pm cut-off (9 January and 23 March were rejected for that reason); in practice R3 flags winter storms only. Coastal flood and high wind warnings are not R3 types, so 9–10 and 13 January and 10 March stay ordinary. A draft analysis outside this repository had already scored March; its worst days were 8, 9 and 16 March. Those results were known when the rules were written, and the parade was on the first candidate list beside them. The rules use outside categories and sources only and were not changed after the dates were looked up. They leave 8 and 9 March ordinary, flag 16 March for its announced closures, and flag 17 March, which was not among the worst. This entry is merged before March is scored in this repository.
- Alternative. Holiday, event and weather predictors in the quantile regression (too few flagged days to estimate them). Keeping flagged days in the fit and offset and reporting them only in the March score (they would shift the allowance for ordinary days). Event hours instead of whole days (closure times are not published for every event). Observed weather instead of warnings (not known before departure). Counting watches and advisories as well as warnings (a looser rule that would flag 16 and 19 January, 17 February, and 6–7 and 23 March). Counting coastal flood and high wind warnings (they would flag 9–10 and 13 January and 10 March; R3 was written for snow, ice and flooding rain).
- Revisit. R2 events were found from NYC closure notices and news coverage, not from a complete register of permits. If a source shows a January–March 2024 event or warning that meets a rule and is missing from `events.py`, add it in a new entry before March is scored. If the March results on flagged days are too few to say anything, report the counts and say so.

## 2026-11-08: Cleaning the records

- Decision. Of 21,726 Midtown-to-JFK records for January–March 2024, drop the 266 reversal copies (negative fare or total), the one non-positive duration, and the 37 trips over six hours, leaving 21,422 (January 6,817; February 6,409; March 8,196). Keep the 45 trips between two and six hours. The over-six-hour trips are left out of the January fit, the February offset and the main March score, and are put back in a sensitivity analysis. The same rules apply to all three months, in `taxi.py`.
- Why. The proposal entry asked for the duplicate candidates and long rides to be investigated before modelling. A reversal is a second copy of a trip with the same times, zones and distance and a negative fare: a voided payment, not a second ride. 265 of the 266 match a positive original exactly; the remaining one (12 February, 3:04 am) has no original in the data and is dropped as a voided record. The 110 rows "in possible duplicate groups" in the 1 November entry are January's 55 pairs. No other duplicates remain. The over-six-hour trips are meters left running, not road time: 34 of 37 are at the $70 JFK flat rate, 35 end on a later date than they began, and their median length is 22.5 hours. Trips of two to six hours could be real traffic, so they stay. The one trip spanning the 2 am clock change on 10 March is one of the over-six-hour trips, so no kept trip is affected. March was used only for these record-quality checks; the six-hour cut-off comes from the proposal entry. The long-trip description and the two-to-six-hour count include its records, but in this repository no median, percentile or score of the kept March trips has been computed (the earlier draft analysis is disclosed in the 2026-11-07 entry).
- Alternative. Dropping both records of each reversal pair (loses 265 real trips). Dropping everything over two hours (would remove possible real delays and flatter both methods). Keeping the over-six-hour trips in the main score (they are not trip durations). The proposal rejected dropping them because they hurt performance; here they are dropped for data quality, and the sensitivity puts them back.
- Revisit. If the TLC documents reversal records or the long drop-off stamps differently, or if the sensitivity changes which method wins on March, report both results; do not change the rule after seeing March.

## 2026-11-08: The split

- Decision. A forward split by pickup time, in New York local time as the TLC records it. Fit: 1 January to 1 February 2024 (6,817 cleaned trips, 6,194 on ordinary days). Calibrate: 1 February to 1 March (6,409 trips, 5,986 on ordinary days), used only for each method's additive offset. Test: 1 March to 1 April (8,196 trips), scored once with the frozen methods. Each cutoff is midnight at the start of the month (`taxi.SPLIT`). No random draw, so no seed.
- Why. The proposal entry fixes this order. The allowance is for future trips, so the test month comes after the fit and calibration months, and the February offset is the proposal's answer to month-to-month drift.
- Alternative. A random split of trips (would put the same days and traffic on both sides). Fitting on January and February together (leaves nothing to calibrate on before March).
- Revisit. If a later month of data is added, test on it rather than re-scoring March.

## 2026-11-10: The quantile regression model

- Decision. Fit Q_0.9(T | h, d, z) = a[w(d), b(h)] + g_d + c_z by quantile regression at tau = 0.90 (statsmodels `QuantReg`, in `model.py`). Weekdays and weekends each get their own hourly profile a: one value per hour from 4 am to 7 pm and one night value for 8 pm to 4 am, the hours with fewer than 100 January trips each. g_d shifts the profile for Tuesday to Friday against Monday and for Sunday against Saturday; c_z shifts it for zones 162, 163 and 230 against 161. That is 42 coefficients. Fit on January ordinary-day trips (6,194), calibrate with the February additive offset on February ordinary-day trips (5,986) using `scoring.february_additive_offset`, the same rule as the fixed allowance, then freeze the coefficients and the offset (+3.83 minutes). March is not read.
- Why. These are the proposal's predictors, all known before departure. The structure was chosen by holding out one January day at a time and scoring it with a model fit on the other 28 days. Separate weekday and weekend profiles score 1.956 against 2.084 for hour, day and zone simply added together (difference −0.128, standard error 0.033, lower on 24 of 29 days): the weekday rush has no weekend counterpart, and an additive model cannot say so. February's numbers were seen beside the January comparison and point the same way. A separate profile for each of the seven days scored 2.143 on the same held-out days (checked outside the notebook), no better than the additive model, because 50 of its 168 hour-by-day cells have fewer than 10 January trips. Pooling the night hours leaves the smallest cell with 27 trips (weekend, 7 pm). In sample, January coverage is 0.897. On February the uncalibrated model covers 0.832 and the offset brings it to 0.900; the allowances run from 30.8 to 88.4 minutes. February quantile loss is 2.259 against 3.462 for the fixed allowance, which is a check and not a test, since both offsets were set on those trips. Uncertainty is from resampling whole days, because trips on one day share its traffic (300 draws, seed 600; January days drawn within each day of the week, February days drawn freely): a trip's allowance has a standard error of about 2.7 minutes (at most 5.9), the model's offset 1.9 minutes, and the fixed allowance 3.2 minutes. After calibration both methods fall short from 4 to 8 pm in February (0.816 for the model, 0.774 for the fixed allowance), and the model covers 0.877 on weekdays against 0.951 at weekends. That is a slower February afternoon, not missing predictors: the 4 to 8 pm 90th percentile rose from 75.9 minutes in January to 85.6 in February while every other period moved by 3 minutes or less, and held-out January days show no such shortfall. The rise sits in two of February's five weeks (weekday 4 to 8 pm 90th percentiles of 92 and 96 minutes in the weeks of 12 and 26 February, 79 to 82 in the others). One offset for the whole month cannot follow it. The proposal's Revisit asked for the predictors or the quantile to be reconsidered if coverage differed sharply by period; they were reconsidered, and neither is the cause.
- Alternative. Hour, day and zone added together with no interaction (worse on held-out January days). A profile for each of the seven days (too many thin cells; no better than the additive model). A smooth daily curve from sines and cosines in place of hourly values (1.942 on the same held-out days, within noise of the chosen 1.956, but its coefficients have no meaning in minutes). A separate February offset for each period or day type (would follow the afternoon shortfall, but it changes the proposal's calibration rule after February was seen, and applied to the baseline it would stop being a fixed allowance). Fitting on January and February together (leaves nothing to calibrate on).
- Revisit. If March shows the afternoon shortfall again, report coverage by departure period and day type for both methods beside the mean loss, and name a per-period offset as the next thing to test on a later month; do not change the model or the offset after seeing March.

## 2026-11-10: The primary outcome, the loss, and how March is scored

- Decision. The primary outcome is the mean quantile loss, L(B, T) = 0.9 max(T − B, 0) + 0.1 max(B − T, 0) in weighted minutes per trip, on March 2024 ordinary-day trips after cleaning, for the two frozen methods: the quantile regression (coefficients and offset of +3.83 from the entry above) and the fixed allowance (76.00 minutes). The reported comparison is the fixed allowance's mean loss minus the model's. The method with the lower mean loss is preferred; if the 90% interval of the difference includes zero, the result is reported as a tie. The interval comes from resampling March ordinary days with replacement, the same days for both methods (10,000 draws, seed 600, 5th to 95th percentile; `evaluation.day_bootstrap_difference`). Beside the loss, each method's coverage, late trips per 100, minutes late when late and minutes unused when on time are reported, and the same table by departure period and by weekday or weekend. Three sensitivities, none of which can change the preferred method: (1) all March days, and the flagged days alone as counts and rates (2026-11-07 entry); (2) the over-six-hour trips put back (`drop_over_6h=False`); (3) a 95% target: both methods refit and recalibrated at tau = 0.95 on the same January and February trips and scored with the 0.95/0.05 loss. March is scored once, by the cells under "March" in `analysis.ipynb`; nothing fitted, calibrated or chosen changes afterwards.
- Why. The proposal entry fixed the loss and named March mean quantile loss as the primary outcome; this entry settles what it left open before March is read. Ordinary days are primary because the allowance is meant for them (2026-11-07 entry) and both methods were fit and calibrated on them. Over-six-hour trips are meters left running (2026-11-08 entry), so they stay out of the main score. The interval resamples days, not trips: on February, treating trips as independent gives the offset a standard error of 0.35 minutes with the model held fixed and resampling days gives 1.60, more than four times larger, so a trip-level interval would make the winner look more certain than it is. Resampling the same days for both methods compares them on the same traffic. The 95% sensitivity uses the matching 0.95/0.05 loss so that each target is scored by the loss it minimises. The minutes behind the loss are reported because a traveller acts on minutes late and minutes waiting, not on weighted minutes.
- Alternative. All March days as the primary outcome (mixes in days the allowance is not meant for). A trip-level standard error or a t-test (ignores that trips on one day move together). Scoring the 95% target with the 0.9/0.1 loss (would favour whichever method happens to sit nearer the 90% allowance). Coverage as the primary outcome (it ignores how late and how much time is unused; a very large allowance wins).
- Revisit. If March has too few flagged-day trips to say anything, report the counts and say so (2026-11-07 entry). If a sensitivity points the other way from the primary outcome, report both and keep the primary outcome. A later month, not March, is where any change is tested.
